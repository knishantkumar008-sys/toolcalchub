EMI Calculator Guide: How to Calculate Loan EMI and Reduce Your Interest Payment

📅 March 2026⏱ 7 min read✍️ ToolCalcHub Team

EMI — Equated Monthly Installment — is the fixed monthly payment you make to repay a loan. Understanding how EMI works helps you choose the right loan tenure, negotiate better rates, and avoid paying more interest than necessary.

The EMI Formula Explained

EMI = P × r × (1+r)ⁿ ÷ ((1+r)ⁿ − 1)

P = Principal loan amount
r = Monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100)
n = Total number of months (tenure)

Example: ₹10 lakh loan at 8.5% for 5 years:
r = 8.5/12/100 = 0.00708
n = 60 months
EMI = ₹20,518/month

How Tenure Affects Total Interest

A longer loan tenure reduces your monthly EMI but dramatically increases total interest paid. Here's an example with a ₹10 lakh loan at 8.5%:

Strategies to Reduce Total Interest

  • Make part-prepayments: Even one extra EMI per year can reduce loan tenure by 2–3 years
  • Choose shorter tenure: If you can afford higher EMI, you save significantly on interest
  • Refinance if rates drop: If interest rates fall by 1%+, refinancing can save thousands
  • Increase EMI annually: As your salary grows, increase EMI by 5–10% each year

EMI Rules of Thumb

Financial advisors generally recommend that your total EMI payments across all loans should not exceed 40–50% of your net monthly take-home pay. For home loans specifically, 30–35% is safer to maintain financial flexibility.
